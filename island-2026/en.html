ABOUT

About the Project

The Island project starts with questions about things that are disappearing. Artists, producers and citizens come together to face disappearance and create poetic spaces within everyday life.

For 2025–2026, the island is Ganghwado. The project began with a research phase in 2025, bringing together artists and producers from South Korea, Singapore and Italy. Walking Ganghwa's paths, visiting historical and cultural sites and meeting its people, each of us began to sense, in our own way, what has been disappearing here.

In 2026, artists from Japan and Malaysia join us. We return to Ganghwa with the questions found last year, to make poetic places where things are vanishing and let them slowly gather on the island.

The two hexagons symbolize the project. Hexagons interlock to create new spaces; each one is both “my island” and “our island.”

This year the project runs on Ganghwa from 25 October to 8 November. Audiences can take part from 6 to 8 November. More details will be added to this page as the project unfolds.

ARTISTS

Participants & Organizations

Artists from Korea, Italy, Singapore, Malaysia and Japan live and work on Ganghwa.

A.N.D. Theatre

GANGHWA, KOREA

KangYun Minji · Kwon Keunyoung · Min Jaewon · Jeon Yunhwan · Jo Naetmul · Byun Junseop

A.N.D. Theatre is an arts company based on Ganghwa Island. Since moving to Ganghwa in 2018, it has created the Void Theatre and the Void Theatre Residency with fellow artists, exploring new relationships between the region and art. Its works include 〈Ganghwa Walk: Peace Document〉 (2023), a walking performance that follows the memories of displaced grandmothers from Gyodong Island, and the 〈Jamsiseom Theatre Festival〉 (2021–), where invited artists stay and make work on the island. Believing in senses that stay alive amid disappearance, the company keeps testing new forms of art on Ganghwa.

Yonrimog

GANGHWA, KOREA

Yonrimog began her music career with the indie rock band “Open your eyes Cobain” and has released albums for more than twenty years. She has raised two children on Ganghwa for ten years, in a life where art and daily life are not separate. After the “TanEmotion” project with Korean traditional instruments and years of scoring dance, theatre and film, she began working under her own name in 2023, including the telephone sound installation 〈Reminiscence of Reminiscence〉. She also runs the street music theatre group “umdalda” with her family. For her, music is an art of time that lingers within disappearance.

Teatringestazione

NAPLES, ITALY

Anna Gesualdi · Giovanni Trono

A multidisciplinary performing arts company founded in Naples in 2006 by Gesualdi | Trono, joined since 2007 by dramaturg Loretta Mesiti. Focused on sharing processes, the company works with people in fragile contexts, including prisons, migrants, the elderly and people with disabilities. Through Altofest, where citizens open their homes to artists for fifteen days, and Ilinea d'Aria (“Air Line Distance”), which reconnected depopulating mountain villages through performance and radio, it treats art as a social act that links people and places again.

Heewarae

GANGHWA, KOREA

Kim Narae · Lee Gunhee

A community-art collective led by visual artists Kim Narae and Lee Gunhee, working in planning, education and running a space since moving from Seoul to Ganghwa. Since 2020 they have turned to people and places that went unseen, such as migrant women, social workers and Ganghwa's dondae (coastal forts). Since 2023, on the small island of Achado, they walk, farm and cook with its aging women residents, practicing art as the “rhythm of daily life.” Their keyword is care: like trees and gardens growing where a fort's stones have crumbled, they look for the life that blossoms within disappearance.

Drama Box (Heng Leun)

SINGAPORE

A socially engaged arts company in Singapore that seeks social change through art. Founder Heng Leun served as artistic director for thirty years before handing the role to fellow artists, and now works as a curator, dramaturg and artistic director. For two decades the company has worked on “land, space and place,” making multilingual performances in libraries, housing estates and streets. Its long-term 〈Project 12〉 takes place on Pulau Ubin, an island twelve minutes by boat from the main island whose population fell from 3,000 to 28, exploring ecology, capital and time with its residents.

Chew Shaw En

SINGAPORE

Shaw is a performing artist, a maker who views the world from the body, and a facilitator of community-based art who connects people, spaces and communities. She works mostly with children from vulnerable families, whom she calls “hidden children,” treating invisibility as a precursor to disappearance. In one Singapore neighbourhood she ran a “Community Performance Night” at the same time and place every week for two and a half years, building a practice of embeddedness in place, practice and people. For her, art is “the most important thing, yet at the same time the least important thing.”

Orang Orang Drum Theatre

PENANG, MALAYSIA

Leow Hui Min · Leow Sze Yee · Chew Soon Heng

Founded in Malaysia in 2013 by Leow Sze Yee (Zyee) and Chew Soon Heng (Boyz Chew). Starting from the folk performance 24 Festive Drums, the group explores drum theatre, in which drummer-performers move between storyteller, musician and physical actor, with the interplay of sound and body as its core language. It has performed at festivals in France, Hungary, Australia, Poland, Taiwan and Korea, and is joined by Leow Hui Min, who studied performing arts in Bordeaux and Venice. Its current focus is the Shan-Ka-La Project, an orchard space that brings farming and performing arts together.

Sononochi

KYOTO, JAPAN

Kazuyo Nakatani · Miho Fujiwara · Moe Nagasawa

A performing arts group founded in Kyoto in 2013. Its head, director and playwright Kazuyo Nakatani, works with artists in music, painting and architecture, treating spaces and bodies like slowly shifting seasons to weave a place's layered stories into the audience's time and memory. In its Landscape Theatre, performers slip into everyday scenes as extraordinary presences, in rural landscapes, galleries and former factory sites. Production manager Moe Nagasawa researches the role of theatres in local communities and their links with social welfare at Doshisha University.

Producer Group DOT

SEOUL, KOREA

Park Jisun · Song Miseon

A collective of performing arts producers formed in 2014, with creativity, independence and horizontality at its core. Alongside domestic productions, international co-productions and tours, it builds process-based work through theme-based research, residencies and networks. From 〈Connected City〉 (2017–2021), which looked at what is disappearing in Seoul's old centre, and 〈DMZ Research〉, to the Artists' Residency – Climate Change with Arts Farm Tutbat in Hwacheon (since 2020), the Ganghwa project continues DOT's exploration of borders, friction, connection, the climate crisis and what is disappearing.
